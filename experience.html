01Futurism TechnologiesSOC Analyst Intern
Hinjewadi Phase 1, I2IT Road, Pune · Jan 2026 – Jul 2026

I worked as a Level-1 analyst in a managed SOC that watches many clients around the clock, deciding which alerts are real attacks and which are just noise.

SOC dashboard · alert mixIllustrative split of the alert types I worked on. Client data stays confidential.
100%of alerts triaged

    Real problems, and how I solved them

    • Thousands of alerts a day, and most are harmless.

      I triaged each alert by severity, source, affected user and device, and the rule that fired, checked that user’s or device’s history, and closed false positives with evidence.

    • A single suspicious event rarely tells the whole story.

      In IBM QRadar I investigated offenses that tie together firewall, server, Active Directory and VPN logs. In Microsoft Sentinel I used KQL to chase cloud sign-in alerts such as impossible travel and risky sign-ins.

    • Is this IP, link or file actually malicious?

      I checked indicators in VirusTotal, AbuseIPDB and Cisco Talos, and detonated suspicious files in Hybrid Analysis and ANY.RUN to see what they really do.

    • Could company credentials or data already be exposed outside the network?

      I used dark web monitoring tools to watch for signs of leaked credentials and data, so exposure could be reported before it was abused.

    • A machine looks compromised. What did the malware do?

      I read process trees, command lines, registry changes and network connections in Defender XDR and Cybereason, and mapped what I saw to MITRE ATT&CK.

    • Phishing is where most attacks begin.

      In Proofpoint I checked sender reputation, SPF, DKIM and DMARC, email headers, links and attachments, and saw how KnowBe4 phishing simulations train users.

    • A real incident has to reach the right people fast.

      I documented the evidence and recommended action under the SOPs and escalated to the L2 team: brute force, privilege escalation, command-and-control traffic and confirmed malware.

    What I delivered

    • Custom correlation rules and detection logic for brute force, lateral movement and data exfiltration, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK.
    • Threat hunts through log telemetry that surfaced indicators of compromise.
    • Incident reports in the PICERL format: findings, containment and lessons learned.
    • DLP policy governance alongside daily monitoring.

    Types of incidents I analysed

    Identity and access brute-force and repeated failed logins, account lockouts, privileged account activity, VPN sign-in anomalies

    Cloud impossible travel, risky sign-ins, new device registrations, Conditional Access alerts, suspicious Azure AD logins

    Endpoint malware detections, ransomware-like behaviour, persistence, lateral movement, suspicious PowerShell

    Network firewall deny events, unusual outbound traffic, command-and-control communication, policy violations

    Email phishing, malicious attachments and links, spam campaigns, impersonation and business email compromise

    What I learned about the job

    A large share of the Sentinel alerts turned out to be false positives. That is why checking evidence before escalating matters: it protects the L2 team’s time for the incidents that count.

    03PSPM TechnologiesPython Developer, Chatbot / GenAI
    Pimpri-Chinchwad, Pune · Jun 2024 – Aug 2024 · Chatbot development using Generative AI

    I built a chatbot that answers questions straight from uploaded PDFs, so people can find one detail in a huge file in seconds.

    RAG pipeline · from PDF to grounded answer
    1. Prepare the document
    UploadStreamlit, one or more PDFs
    ExtractPyPDF2 reads every page
    Chunk10,000 characters, 1,000 overlap
    EmbedGoogle Generative AI embeddings
    IndexSaved locally in FAISS
    2. Answer a question
    QuestionPlain English from the user
    RetrieveSimilarity search for the best chunks
    Gemini ProPrompt: answer only from context
    AnswerShown with chat history

    Real problems, and how I solved them

    • Finding one fact inside long PDFs is slow.

      A Streamlit page lets the user upload PDFs and ask in plain English. PyPDF2 pulls out the text.

    • An AI model cannot read a whole large document at once.

      The text is cut into 10,000-character chunks with 1,000 overlap, so nothing is lost at the edges. Each chunk becomes a Google Generative AI embedding in a local FAISS index, and only the most relevant chunks are fetched for each question.

    • AI models make up answers when they are unsure.

      My prompt tells Gemini Pro to answer only from the supplied context and to say the answer is not available if it is not there. I also kept the temperature low.

    • Users ask follow-ups and need to review earlier answers.

      The session keeps the full chat history on screen, and the API key stays in an environment variable instead of the code.

    How I chose the tools

    I explored Pinecone, ChromaDB and FAISS, plus Hugging Face models and OpenAI. I used FAISS locally because it gave fast similarity search with no extra service to run.
